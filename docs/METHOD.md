# Method of the alignment account (pre-registered, v0)

This file fixes the method before outcome data (measured remanence and texture of
specific magnets) are collected. Later changes are made only by commits that say why,
and are tracked in `REPORT.md` (frontier history).

## 1. What is predicted

For a hard phase *p* made by a route *r*, the remanence along the magnet axis **z** at 300 K:

    Br = Js · φ · A

* `Js` — saturation polarisation μ0Ms of the hard phase at 300 K (for two-phase
  shape-anisotropy alloys such as AlNiCo: of the alloy).
* `φ` — volume fraction of the hard phase in the magnet (porosity, binder, other phases).
* `A = Σ_i w_i |ê_i · z|` — the alignment factor: volume-weighted mean |cos| between the
  easy direction ê_i of each crystal (or of each elongated precipitate, for shape
  anisotropy) and z. This is the Stoner–Wohlfarth remanence of non-interacting uniaxial
  units saturated along z. Exchange-coupling remanence enhancement in very fine isotropic
  grains is not alignment and is excluded from A (reported separately where relevant).

Reference values the engine must reproduce: random uniaxial A = 1/2; random grains each
keeping the one of three orthogonal easy axes nearest to z, A = 0.8312 (the cubic K1>0
remanence); perfect alignment A = 1.

**Ceiling.** `A_max(p, r)` is the largest A that route r allows for phase p when every step
is taken at its physical best (perfect parent texture, complete variant selection where a
bias can act, perfect rotation where bodies are free to rotate). `Br_max = Js · A_max · φ_max`
with `φ_max = 1` unless the route's mechanism itself requires another phase (then the
lever-rule fraction from the phase diagram).

**Conditional prediction.** For a published experiment, the same engine run with the
conditions that experiment reports (parent texture if measured, field on/off and its
temperature relative to Tc, etc.). Unreported continuous parameters are bounded, giving an
interval `[A_lo, A_hi]`. No parameter is fitted to outcome data.

## 2. One engine for all phases

A population of crystal orientations (rotation matrices g, weights w) is pushed through
operators. The same operators and rules are applied to every phase; only sourced phase
data enter.

**Texture source S** (orientation distribution of the phase that the hard phase forms
from, or of the hard phase itself when it forms directly):

| code | source | best case |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | none: equiaxed casting, sintering of random powder | random |
| S1 | DS: directional solidification, columnar casting, Bridgman | perfect fibre of the growth direction d_g |
| S2 | AM: additive manufacturing (L-PBF, EBM, DED) | perfect fibre of d_g along the build direction |
| S3 | RX: rolling/drawing (+ recrystallisation) sheet or wire texture | perfect sheet/wire texture of the parent |
| S4 | SC: single crystal or epitaxial film | one orientation |
| S5 | HD: hot deformation of the hard phase itself | perfect version of the deformation fibre the mechanism produces |

**Transformation T** (parent → hard phase), from the published orientation relationship
(OR): each parent orientation gives the variants `g·V_k`, `V_k` generated by the OR and the
parent/product point groups. If the transformation proceeds by incoherent (massive) growth
across parent grains, the product orientation is random. Twins in the product add
variants related by the published twin operations.

**Bias B during formation** (variant weights):

* B0 none — equal weights.
* B1 field — weights favour variants whose easy axis is nearest the field. It acts only if
  the forming phase is ferromagnetic at the formation temperature T_f (Tc > T_f). Above Tc
  the bias is the paramagnetic anisotropy energy `μ0 Δχ H² V*/2`, compared with kT for the
  volume V* that decides the variant (a nucleus); if ≪ kT the weights stay equal.
* B2 stress — weights favour variants whose transformation strain does the most work
  against the applied stress; acts for coherent transformations with a non-cubic
  transformation strain, at any magnetic state.

In the ceiling, a bias that acts gives complete selection; a bias that cannot act gives
equal weights.

**Free-body rotation R** — bodies free to rotate (powder in a die or slurry, particles in a
colloid, crystals in a melt) turn their net easy axis to the field. A body containing
variant fractions f_v contributes `Σ_v f_v |ê_v · ê_body|`; a single-variant body
contributes 1. Requires anisotropy energy × body volume ≫ kT and mobility.

**Consolidation** — the hard phase must survive densification: the densification
temperature must stay below the phase's stability limit T_stab, else φ is limited to
what can be reached below T_stab (bonding, warm compaction).

**Routes** are combinations: FP = R on powder (+ consolidation); FS = R on crystals in a
melt; DS = S1+B0; AM = S2+B0; FA = S0+B1; DS+FA, AM+FA, RX+FA, SC+FA; SA = S0+B2 (and with
S1–S4); HD = S5; EP = S4 by epitaxy (films; not a bulk route); TGG = growth on aligned
templates. Every route is evaluated for every phase; a route whose prerequisites fail is
reported as "cannot align" with the failing prerequisite named.

The best magnet axis z is chosen per route (fibre axis or perpendicular, or the best
direction for a single orientation), and stated.

## 3. Inputs

Per phase, each with a named source and a verbatim quote: Js, K1, Tc, density, crystal
structure and easy axis, T_stab, formation path(s) (parent, T_f, mechanism, OR, product
tetragonality relative to the parent), twin operations, preferred growth directions,
whether single-variant particles can be made. Missing inputs are not guessed: a cell
whose verdict depends on a missing input is reported with both outcomes and the
measurement that would settle it.

## 4. Tests

Each prediction comes with one measurement that would confirm or refute it: the quantity
(usually Mr/Ms along z after saturation, or an easy-axis pole figure converted to A), the
sample to make, and the threshold beyond which the prediction is refuted.

## 5. Accuracy

Outcome data are measured remanence (Br with Js and φ, or Mr/Ms) and texture (pole
figures, variant fractions) from published work, each with a source and evidence level.

* Ceiling validity: A_meas ≤ A_max + 2σ for every case (a violation refutes the ceiling).
* Conditional accuracy: fraction of cases with A_meas inside `[A_lo − 2σ, A_hi + 2σ]`,
  reported with the mean interval width (sharpness) so wide intervals cannot score well
  silently.
* Tightness on mature industrial routes: best A_meas / A_max.

**Held-back cases.** Outcome data other than the four known cases named in PROBLEM.md are
collected into sealed files, committed encrypted with their SHA-256, and opened only
after the predictions they test have been committed. Accuracy is reported separately for
the known (fidelity) cases, the development cases and each sealed batch.

## 6. Frontier

Three criteria, each with a limit set outside this work: phases covered (of the phases
named as rare-earth-free candidates in published reviews), route classes covered (of the
routes used or proposed in the literature), and predictive accuracy (conditional accuracy
over all measured cases). The non-dominated versions are listed with their commits;
after each success the criterion furthest from its limit is pushed next.

## 7. Limits

A remaining gap is accepted only if it is physics, or a quantity that is unpublished and
shown (by running the engine on both of its possible values) to decide the answer,
together with the measurement that would settle it.

## 8. Changes made before any sealed outcome was opened

Each change below was committed together with the frozen predictions (data/curated/cases.yaml)
and the scoring code (src/align/scoring.py), before `tools/seal.py open` was run for any batch.

1. **Route classes.** Besides the classes of section 2: MO (shape orientation of single-crystal
   particles by pressing/flow), SC+FA and SC+SA (bias on a single-crystal parent), DS+SA and
   RX+SA (stress on a textured parent), HDDR, TGG. 18 classes in all, every one evaluated for
   every phase.
2. **Field rule.** The field acts if the nucleus that decides the variant is magnetically
   ordered at T_f: product Tc > T_f, or a ferromagnetic parent with Tc > T_f polarising a
   ferromagnetic product. If the Curie point of the forming phase is unpublished the cell is
   open (both outcomes given).
3. **Unpublished inputs** are never guessed: the engine is run on each possible value and the
   cell lists every outcome (verdict 'open' only if the outcomes fall in different categories).
   An unpublished orientation relationship is expanded into two hypotheses (no OR / the stated
   analogue OR); an unpublished easy direction makes every ceiling conditional on it.
4. **Free bodies** with a degenerate anisotropy tensor (e.g. three equal orthogonal variants)
   have no unique axis: the field direction is spread over the degenerate subspace.
5. **Two-step transformations** (parent -> intermediate -> product) choose variants at two
   levels; each level acts only if its deciding phase is ordered (field) or coherent (stress).
6. **Unbiased spinodal alloys (AlNiCo, Fe-Cr-Co, alnico-like HEA).** Without a bias the
   ferromagnetic precipitates lie along all three parent <100>. Two readings bracket the
   remanence: independent rods (A_base: 1/2 for random grains) and one coupled network per
   grain with the published no-field easy directions <111> (A_hi: 0.866 for random grains;
   Nesbitt 1952, record alnico5_field_easyaxis_nesbitt1952). Ties of the nearest-<100> rule
   (field along <111> or <110>) are given both readings as well.
7. **Measuring direction off the bias axis.** Partial selection can exceed complete selection
   there, so the ceiling is the larger of the complete-selection and the unbiased values.
8. **Case predictions** (data/curated/cases.yaml) replace the [A_lo, A_hi] of section 1 by:
   A_hi (ceiling under the case's own conditions), A_base (the same without the route's
   mechanism), a class ('acts', 'none', 'open', or 'info' = not a test of alignment), optional
   hypothesis values, the conversion of the measured quantity to A (Js from a same-sample
   record, a sourced grade interval, or the phase Js -/+ 5 %; phi where the hard phase is
   diluted), and optional pairs (same study, one condition changed) with the expected sign of
   the change. The interval of section 5 is kept as [min(A_base, A_hi) - tol, A_hi + tol].
9. **Scoring** (src/align/scoring.py, docstring): tol = max(0.03, 2x stated uncertainty);
   a ceiling is violated only if the most favourable conversion of the measurement still
   exceeds it (straddling cases are listed as possible violations); 'none' is refuted by
   A_meas > A_hi + tol; 'acts' is shown by A_meas > A_base + tol (not shown is not a
   refutation); each hypothesis of an open case is kept or refuted; pairs are checked for the
   predicted sign. Known cases (PROBLEM.md) are scored with the same rules and reported
   separately as fidelity; exposed cases (docs/PROTOCOL_LOG.md) are reported separately from
   held-out ones.

## 9. Changes made after the first sealed batches were opened

Listed item by item, with their triggers, in docs/PROTOCOL_LOG.md (post-hoc revision v2). They
are scored separately and never as held-out; v2 is held-out only on batches opened after its
freeze. Additions that do not change any earlier prediction:

1. **Cubic phases with several easy directions** (Mn4N, <111>): each grain keeps the easy
   direction nearest the measuring axis, so a random polycrystal has A = 0.866 and alignment can
   only raise A to 1; cells use that value instead of the uniaxial 1/2.
2. **Epitaxy** is a film route: its ceiling is the geometric value of a single-variant film
   (A = 1, or less with several in-plane variants); whether a film has been grown is stated, not
   used as a limit.
3. **Open cells** list first the unpublished quantity whose possible values change the ceiling;
   if two hypotheses give the same ceiling, the cell is settled on it.
4. **Scoring statuses**: 'post hoc' marks cases scored with rules revised after their outcomes
   were seen.
